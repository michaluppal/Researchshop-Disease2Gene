\section{Motivation and Significance}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{The Knowledge-Extraction Bottleneck}

In this paper, we present ResearchShop, a text-mining solution for auditable gene and variant extraction from biomedical literature.

At the start of a literature-based project, researchers must determine which papers are relevant, which genes and variants recur, and where the supporting evidence appears. ResearchShop automates this first-pass mapping. From a documented PubMed query or selected paper set, it produces a structured, source-linked overview for further expert review. The software supports orientation in a research topic; it does not determine biological importance or clinical significance. Each run is personalisable: users choose a PubMed query or paper set and define the qualitative variables of interest that the pipeline should extract as a user-defined schema, rather than returning a fixed, one-size-fits-all summary.

ResearchShop was developed from practical need during work on paediatric inflammatory multisystem syndrome (PIMS), also called multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C)~\cite{who2020misc}, where immune findings and genetic evidence are distributed across full text, figures, tables, and supplementary material rather than concentrated in abstracts. The practical requirement is to recover gene names together with the surrounding evidence, provenance, and validation context needed for expert review.

The tool addresses a literature-scale bottleneck that extends beyond variant interpretation. Sequencing and publication volume are both high, and assessing findings often requires aggregating evidence scattered across papers, figures, and tables. PubMed indexes more than 40 million citations and abstracts in biomedical and life-science literature~\cite{pubmed_about_2026}. Curated resources such as ClinVar and Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM) provide essential interpretation records built from submitted, reviewed, and periodically updated evidence~\cite{landrum2025clinvar,amberger2015omim}, but they do not summarise a live paper set or recover the associations a researcher has defined for a given question. ResearchShop supports that upstream step: it aggregates selected open-access papers into a structured, source-linked overview and extracts user-specified gene--disease associations, including disease--protein, and protein--protein interactions, in cases when gene and protein name is identical.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Approaches}
Existing tools either tag gene and variant mentions or ask an LLM to extract associations. NER-based solutions find and normalize mentions; LLMs can fill user-defined fields from context. Neither approach alone is sufficient to create summary further inspection and review of the process.

Dedicated systems such as PubTator Central~\cite{wei2019pubtatorcentral}, BioBERT~\cite{lee2020biobert}, scispaCy~\cite{neumann2019scispacy}, and tmVar~3.0~\cite{wei2022tmvar3} are strong at entity recognition and normalization, but they do not by themselves provide schema-driven, source-linked extraction. LLM workflows can recover richer associations between genes and findings, but they require explicit safeguards against hallucinations~\cite{ji2023hallucination,farquhar2024semanticentropy}. ResearchShop therefore uses NER and deterministic HGNC scanning to propose source-grounded candidates, then asks an LLM to fill a user-defined schema for those candidates. Validation layers assess each row and flag it for export or removal. Table~\ref{tab:tool_comparison} summarises this comparison. Two limitations motivate this architecture:

\paragraph{Full-text accessibility} Abstract-only mining misses information that appears in Results, Methods, tables, captions, and supplementary material. PubTator Central reported that concept taggers recovered 56--65\% of manual annotations from abstracts but 78--83\% from full text, an 18--22 percentage-point increase~\cite{wei2019pubtatorcentral}. ResearchShop therefore processes open-access full text: it fetches PMC and Europe PMC documents~\cite{europepmc2015} and uses body text, tables, figure captions, and available figure and supplementary material, not abstracts alone. Papers without retrievable open-access full text are excluded from automated extraction; no paywall bypass is attempted.

\paragraph{LLM hallucinations} LLMs can support information extraction but are prone to generating plausible yet factually incorrect associations~\cite{farquhar2024semanticentropy}. Hallucinations in this setting include invented identifiers, such as a non-HGNC-approved ``\textit{BRCA3}'' label, and real gene symbols recalled from model pre-training but absent from the paper being processed. Pure LLM extraction can therefore introduce unsupported false positives, while overly strict validation can create false negatives by excluding real but weakly grounded findings. For biomedical review workflows, each exported association must be tied to a validated identifier and a recoverable source passage.
